# MDOP — Conversion Automotive Web Design Skill

> Project: **MDOP — Make Dreams. Own Porsche.**  
> Type: **Conversion / Commerce Experience**  
> Industry: **Automotive / Luxury Performance**  
> Purpose: Strategic design contract to be used alongside Impeccable before and during implementation.

---

## 1. Core Objective

MDOP is a conversion-first automotive website.

Its primary goal is:

> **Turn product desire into a confident next action without sacrificing premium automotive presentation.**

Priority order:

1. Product clarity
2. Conversion clarity
3. Vehicle desirability
4. Decision confidence
5. Product discovery
6. Friction reduction
7. Visual craft
8. Motion
9. Brand theater

Do not optimize MDOP like a cinematic portfolio.

Do not optimize MDOP like a generic ecommerce store.

It must feel like premium automotive commerce.

---

## 2. Core Conversion Idea

### THE PRODUCT STAYS DESIRABLE. THE NEXT STEP STAYS OBVIOUS.

Every major surface should combine two layers:

**EMOTIONAL LAYER**

- vehicle imagery,
- composition,
- scale,
- material,
- light,
- motion,
- typography.

**DECISION LAYER**

- model identity,
- price position,
- key attributes,
- comparison,
- availability,
- configuration,
- primary CTA.

Neither layer should destroy the other.

---

## 3. Conversion Model

Use this funnel:

**ATTENTION**

↓

**INTEREST**

↓

**DISCOVERY**

↓

**EVALUATION**

↓

**CONFIDENCE**

↓

**INTENT**

↓

**ACTION**

Each section must declare which stage it primarily serves.

If a section does not strengthen one stage, remove or redesign it.

---

## 4. Primary User Questions

The interface must repeatedly help answer:

- Which Porsche is for me?
- What makes this model different?
- What does it cost?
- Is it practical for my needs?
- Can I compare it?
- Can I personalize it?
- Is one available now?
- What happens if I click the CTA?
- Can I save this and return later?

Design around these questions rather than around component categories.

---

## 5. Primary Conversion Actions

Priority actions:

1. **Explore models**
2. **View model**
3. **Configure**
4. **Compare**
5. **Check availability**
6. **Save**
7. **Book test drive**
8. **Request consultation**
9. **Reserve / Begin purchase**

Never visually promote all of them at once.

Each surface needs a primary action.

---

## 6. CTA Hierarchy

### Primary CTA

High commitment or main journey progression.

Examples:

- Explore models
- Configure
- Check availability
- Reserve

### Secondary CTA

Alternative decision support.

Examples:

- Compare
- Save
- View details
- Talk to advisor

### Tertiary CTA

Low-emphasis informational action.

Examples:

- Learn more
- View gallery
- Ownership details

Use hierarchy through:

- contrast,
- placement,
- size,
- spacing,
- repetition discipline.

Do not solve hierarchy by making every button louder.

---

## 7. Homepage Conversion Narrative

### Stage 01 — Desire + Entry

Show a high-impact Porsche visual.

Immediately offer:

**Explore models**

and one secondary path:

**Build your Porsche**

No long cinematic intro.

---

### Stage 02 — Product Range

Show recognizable model families.

The user should understand the range at a glance.

Each model family needs:

- name,
- visual,
- use-case cue,
- price field,
- action.

---

### Stage 03 — Guided Choice

Introduce:

**Find Your Porsche**

Help the user reduce choice overload.

Use questions that materially change recommendations.

---

### Stage 04 — Product Desire

Give one featured model a more cinematic moment.

This is where stronger art direction is welcome.

Keep product information and CTA visible.

---

### Stage 05 — Compare

Give uncertain users a structured decision tool.

Comparison should emphasize differences rather than repeat identical data.

---

### Stage 06 — Available Now

Serve high-intent users.

Inventory should become more transactional and scan-friendly.

---

### Stage 07 — Ownership Confidence

Reduce anxiety around:

- service,
- warranty,
- finance,
- charging,
- delivery,
- support.

---

### Stage 08 — Decision

End with one strong action.

Example:

**Build your Porsche**

Do not end with a giant menu of choices.

---

## 8. Product Page Contract

Every vehicle detail page should satisfy this order:

1. Identify the product.
2. Show it beautifully.
3. Establish price position.
4. Establish primary benefits.
5. Present the next action.
6. Prove relevant differences.
7. Address uncertainty.
8. Repeat the action at meaningful points.

Primary CTA should remain accessible during long exploration.

---

## 9. Product Hero Rules

The first viewport should contain:

- model name,
- product image,
- concise positioning,
- price/demo-price field,
- primary CTA,
- one secondary CTA.

Avoid:

- marketing paragraph walls,
- hidden price,
- CTA below several viewports,
- giant decorative typography that competes with the car.

---

## 10. Product Card Rules

Product cards are allowed because MDOP includes product browsing.

But they must be purposeful.

A product card should include only decision-relevant information.

Recommended:

- high-quality image,
- model,
- short variant/use-case descriptor,
- price field,
- 2–3 meaningful attributes,
- action.

Avoid:

- icon clutter,
- multiple badges,
- excessive metadata,
- nested cards,
- equal-weight CTA clusters.

---

## 11. Configurator Contract

The configurator must prioritize:

**VEHICLE → CHOICE → CONSEQUENCE**

When an option changes:

- the selection is visually confirmed,
- vehicle imagery updates where practical,
- summary updates,
- price/demo-price changes visibly,
- the user remains oriented.

Desktop:

Vehicle canvas may remain sticky.

Mobile:

Controls and vehicle preview should alternate intelligently.

Never force the desktop two-column configurator onto a narrow screen.

---

## 12. Comparison Contract

Maximum recommended:

**2–3 vehicles.**

Comparison should highlight meaningful differences.

Use progressive disclosure.

Default view:

- price,
- body/use case,
- drivetrain,
- seats,
- performance category,
- efficiency/range category,
- cargo/practicality,
- key differentiator.

Allow deeper technical expansion.

Do not begin with dozens of rows.

---

## 13. Inventory Contract

Inventory is a high-intent surface.

Reduce visual theater.

Prioritize:

- vehicle,
- exact variant,
- price,
- availability,
- configuration,
- location/status,
- next action.

Filters must be fast and understandable.

Use sticky filters on desktop only when useful.

Mobile filters should use an accessible sheet/drawer.

---

## 14. Lead Form Contract

Before opening a form, the CTA must explain intent.

Good:

**Book a test drive**

**Ask about this vehicle**

**Request a consultation**

Bad:

**Submit**

**Continue**

unless context already makes the result obvious.

Forms should request the minimum information needed for the simulated follow-up.

---

## 15. Trust Design

Trust should come from evidence and clarity.

Possible trust mechanisms:

- transparent pricing fields,
- clear availability state,
- ownership support,
- warranty information,
- service information,
- process explanation,
- verified data labels,
- clear success states.

Do not use:

- fake scarcity,
- fake reviews,
- fake counters,
- fake social proof,
- pressure countdowns.

Premium conversion should feel confident, not desperate.

---

## 16. Visual Language

MDOP should feel:

**PORSCHE PRODUCT DESIGN × EDITORIAL COMMERCE × PRECISION UI**

Keywords:

- precise,
- premium,
- restrained,
- engineered,
- desirable,
- tactile,
- fast,
- confident.

Avoid:

- generic ecommerce look,
- SaaS dashboard look,
- futuristic HUD,
- excessive neon,
- glassmorphism,
- card overload,
- excessive gradients.

---

## 17. Brand Asset Direction

For this portfolio/design-study project, use high-fidelity Porsche visual material when intentionally provided or sourced for the prototype.

Allowed design direction:

- Porsche crest/logo as a brand cue,
- Porsche vehicle imagery,
- recognizable model photography,
- high-quality interior/exterior details.

Do not distort logos or use low-resolution brand assets.

The visual quality bar must be consistent with the product.

---

## 18. Color Strategy

Foundation:

- tinted near-black,
- warm off-white,
- graphite,
- metallic neutrals.

Accent:

- restrained performance red.

Contextual color:

- selected vehicle exterior paint.

The UI should not compete with product paint colors.

---

## 19. Typography Strategy

Use two systems.

### Display

For:

- hero,
- model identity,
- editorial moments.

Character:

- strong,
- precise,
- architectural,
- high-performance.

### Interface

For:

- prices,
- filters,
- forms,
- configuration,
- technical data,
- navigation.

Character:

- legible,
- neutral,
- premium,
- compact where appropriate.

Do not use over-expressive typography in transactional areas.

---

## 20. Layout Strategy

Do not build every page from identical stacked sections.

Use different composition modes depending on intent.

### Desire mode

- full bleed,
- large imagery,
- asymmetric type,
- controlled whitespace.

### Evaluation mode

- structured grid,
- data hierarchy,
- comparison.

### Action mode

- compact,
- high clarity,
- visible CTA,
- low friction.

The page may shift between these modes deliberately.

---

## 21. Motion Strategy

Target:

**M3 / selective M4**

Motion can:

- reveal the vehicle,
- transition variants,
- communicate selected options,
- reinforce hierarchy,
- preserve spatial continuity.

Motion must not:

- delay CTA access,
- trap scroll,
- create unnecessary waiting,
- move buttons away from the pointer,
- hide product data,
- cause layout instability.

No bounce/elastic visual language.

---

## 22. Responsive Strategy

For every major surface define independently:

- dominant visual,
- CTA placement,
- product info order,
- filter behavior,
- comparison behavior,
- sticky behavior,
- configurator behavior.

Mobile should be able to complete the full funnel.

Not merely browse it.

---

## 23. Conversion Copy Rules

Copy should be:

- concise,
- specific,
- useful,
- premium,
- benefit-aware.

Prefer:

**Explore all models**

over:

**Discover a world of extraordinary mobility**

Prefer:

**Check availability**

over:

**Start your journey**

Use emotional language in brand moments.

Use concrete language in decision moments.

---

## 24. Product Data Rules

Do not invent exact product facts.

Structured product data should support:

```txt
status: verified | demo | unknown
```

During development, any unverified data must remain obvious internally.

The visual design should not depend on fake numbers to feel premium.

---

## 25. State Design

Every interactive surface needs states.

### Buttons

- default,
- hover,
- focus,
- pressed,
- disabled,
- loading.

### Product options

- available,
- selected,
- unavailable,
- incompatible.

### Inventory

- available,
- reserved,
- incoming,
- unavailable.

### Forms

- empty,
- editing,
- invalid,
- submitting,
- success,
- error.

### Saved state

- unsaved,
- saved,
- syncing if used.

No polished surface is complete without state design.

---

## 26. Sticky UI Policy

Sticky UI is permitted when it reduces friction.

Good:

- model + price + Configure on PDP,
- configuration summary,
- mobile bottom CTA,
- compare selector.

Bad:

- multiple sticky bars,
- large sticky hero sections that obscure content,
- CTA overlays covering vehicle controls.

Keep one dominant sticky conversion layer at a time.

---

## 27. Search & Filter Philosophy

Search and filters exist to reduce decision time.

Filter labels should use user language.

Good:

- SUV
- Electric
- 4 seats+
- Available now

Avoid internal automotive jargon unless the audience requires it.

Filters should update results without disorientation.

---

## 28. Performance Discipline

Conversion and perceived speed are connected.

Prioritize:

- fast LCP,
- responsive media,
- image preload discipline,
- minimized JS,
- lazy non-critical modules,
- efficient state management,
- no heavy WebGL unless conversion value is clear.

A slow configurator is a broken configurator.

---

## 29. Accessibility Discipline

Required:

- keyboard-completable journeys,
- proper form semantics,
- focus management,
- accessible filters,
- accessible dialogs,
- screen-reader state announcements,
- reduced motion,
- sufficient contrast,
- non-color-only selection states.

Accessibility is part of product quality.

## 31. Impeccable Init Direction

When `/impeccable init` asks about the surface, treat MDOP as a **brand/marketing commerce surface with product-like transactional interactions**.

The root context should explicitly say:

- product discovery is primary,
- conversion is primary,
- configurator/compare/filter flows behave like product UI,
- cinematic craft is secondary to purchase clarity.

Do not allow init-generated generic marketing conventions to overwrite this contract.

## 33. Funnel Review Questions

For every major screen ask:

### Intent
What did the user probably come here to do?

### Information
What must they know before acting?

### Friction
What could make them hesitate?

### Hierarchy
What is the primary action?

### Proof
What increases confidence?

### Continuity
Does the next screen preserve context?

### Recovery
Can the user go back or change a choice safely?

### Mobile
Can the same goal be completed comfortably on a phone?

---

## 34. Anti-Patterns

Do not create:

- YDAP-style cinematic intro before shopping,
- generic Shopify layout,
- SaaS navigation,
- bento-grid product discovery,
- every model in identical rounded cards,
- fake pricing,
- fake scarcity,
- fake reviews,
- hidden costs,
- CTA overload,
- aggressive popup lead forms,
- auto-playing sound,
- sticky UI everywhere,
- endless technical tables,
- configuration with unclear selected state,
- mobile comparison that requires microscopic horizontal reading.

---

## 35. Design Process

Before coding a major page:

1. Define the user intent.
2. Define the funnel stage.
3. Define the primary CTA.
4. Define required decision information.
5. Define objections/friction.
6. Define the visual moment.
7. Define responsive behavior.
8. Define states.
9. Define analytics events.
10. Only then implement.

Do not begin from components.

Begin from:

**Intent → Decision → Conversion → Composition → Interaction → Components**

---

## 36. Homepage Creative Directions

Before implementation, produce 3 genuinely different conversion-oriented directions.

Each direction must define:

- visual metaphor,
- homepage funnel,
- model discovery behavior,
- product-card language,
- hero CTA architecture,
- featured-product treatment,
- motion level,
- mobile strategy,
- conversion risks.

The three directions must differ structurally, not merely by color.

Select one and write it into `DESIGN.md`.

## 38. Final Principle

YDAP optimized:

**ATTENTION → EMOTION → MEMORY**

MDOP optimizes:

**DESIRE → DISCOVERY → CONFIDENCE → ACTION**

The visual design must still make the Porsche feel special.

But the product experience must make the decision feel easy.

> **THE CAR CREATES DESIRE.  
> THE INTERFACE CREATES CONFIDENCE.  
> THE NEXT STEP CREATES CONVERSION.**

**MDOP**
